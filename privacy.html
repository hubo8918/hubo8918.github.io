calsync ("the app") is an open-source, self-hosted tool that mirrors one Apple iCloud calendar into a Google Calendar. This policy explains what data the app touches, where that data lives, and what it is used for.

The short version: calsync runs entirely on hardware you control. It has no backend service, no hosted component, and no analytics. The developer of calsync never receives, stores, or has any access to your calendar data, your Google account data, or your credentials.

1. Data the app accesses

Google account data

calsync requests the following Google OAuth scope:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar — used to read the calendar list entry for the destination calendar (to confirm it exists and that you have write access), to subscribe to that calendar if it is not already in your list, and to create, update, and delete the mirrored events in that one calendar.

Through this scope the app reads and writes event data — titles, descriptions, locations, start and end times, recurrence rules, and the private metadata marker calsync attaches to events it created — in the single destination calendar you configure. It does not read or modify any other calendar in your account.

Apple iCloud data

calsync connects to iCloud over CalDAV using an Apple ID and an app-specific password that you supply in your own configuration. It performs read-only requests and copies event fields from the source calendar. Attendee and organizer information is deliberately excluded and never sent to Google.

Credentials and tokens

Your Google OAuth client secret and the resulting access and refresh tokens, plus your iCloud app-specific password, are stored in files and environment variables on the machine where you run calsync. They are transmitted only to Google and Apple over HTTPS, as required to perform the sync.

2. How the data is used

Calendar data is used for exactly one purpose: keeping the destination Google Calendar in sync with the source iCloud calendar. The data is not used for advertising, profiling, training machine learning or AI models, or any purpose unrelated to the sync feature you configured.

3. Where the data is stored

All local state — the sync token, the mapping between iCloud events and Google events, content hashes, and the OAuth token file — is written to a data directory on your own machine or server. There is no calsync server. No data is uploaded to the developer or to any third party other than Google (Google Calendar API) and Apple (iCloud CalDAV), which are the two services you are explicitly connecting.

4. Data sharing

calsync does not sell, rent, or share your data with anyone. No human associated with the project can read your calendar data, because the project operates no infrastructure that receives it.

5. Limited Use disclosure

calsync's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Specifically, data obtained through Google Calendar APIs is used only to provide and improve the calendar sync feature, is not transferred to others except as necessary to provide that feature or as required by law, is not used for advertising, and is not used to develop, improve, or train generalized AI or ML models.

6. Data retention and deletion

You are in full control of retention, because all data is stored on your own system:

  • Delete the app's data directory (including the stored OAuth token file) to remove all locally cached calendar data and credentials.
  • Revoke calsync's access to your Google account at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Revocation takes effect immediately and the stored refresh token stops working.
  • Revoke the iCloud app-specific password at account.apple.com.
  • Delete the mirrored events, or the whole destination calendar, in Google Calendar. Because the sync is one-way, this never affects your iCloud calendar.

7. Security

All connections to Google and Apple use HTTPS. Credentials are kept in local configuration and token files rather than being embedded in the code. Because calsync is self-hosted, the security of the machine it runs on, and of the files holding your credentials, is your responsibility.

8. Children

calsync is a developer tool and is not directed at children under 13.
